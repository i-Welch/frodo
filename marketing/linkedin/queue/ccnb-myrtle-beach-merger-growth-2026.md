Coastal Carolina National Bank opened in June 2009, in a coastal resort market getting crushed by the financial crisis. On October 1 it closed a merger of equals that created a $2.3 billion South Carolina bank.

The numbers going in were strong. First-half 2026 net income of $6.82 million, up 26%. A 1.05% ROA. Efficiency down to 59.17% from 61.78% a year earlier. Zero noncurrent loans.

Now comes the hard part. Sixteen branches and two legacy workflows run side by side until core systems convert in early 2027, and the whole bank rebrands as Beacon Bank, N.A. in mid-March.

Banks that come out of a conversion in good shape standardize their critical workflows before the new core goes live, not after. In mortgage, the highest-friction step is verification: income, employment and assets.

CCNB's borrowers make that harder, not easier. In its 2024 HMDA data the largest borrower income band is $250,000-plus, with $150,000 to $250,000 close behind, many of them retirees and remote workers relocating with complex income.

Which workflows should a merging bank standardize before conversion day?
