First Capital Bank in Charleston grew 25% in a year, from $1.05 billion to $1.32 billion in assets, with no online loan application.

Updated with its Q2 2026 call report, the numbers are strong:

ROA of 1.14%, up from 0.88% a year earlier.
An efficiency ratio of 53.34%, down from 59.8%.
Net loans of $1.08 billion, about 58% of it commercial: nonfarm CRE, construction, multifamily and C&I.

And every loan still starts the same way. The loans page tells mortgage applicants to email or call a lender.

That model built a $1.3 billion bank. The question is whether it scales in a metro adding roughly 17,500 residents a year, most of them relocating from out of state with out-of-market employers and financial histories spread across other banks.

Those are the borrowers a manual document chase handles worst, and the ones digital-first lenders are built to win.

Can a relationship bank keep growing on phone-and-email intake once the market outpaces it?
