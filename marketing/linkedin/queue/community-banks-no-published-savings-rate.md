18 of the 21 community banks we checked in the Southeast publish no savings rate on their own websites.

We were adding the largest community banks in SC, NC, GA and FL to our savings account scorecard, working down each state's list by assets. The scorecard only ranks rates it can read on an institution's own pages.

The savings pages list fees, minimums and features. Then they say "competitive", or "variable", or call your local branch. One online account opening flow lists the rate as "TBD".

The credit unions went the other way. 13 of the 14 we could read publish a savings rate, conditions included.

Three of them pay over 3% for a small monthly deposit. Skyla Credit Union pays 4.10% on $10,000 with $25 a month and eStatements. Sharonview pays 3.20%. Georgia United pays 3.15%, and 1.75% with no conditions at all.

None of this means the unpublished rates are bad. It means a saver comparing accounts online, or asking an AI assistant, cannot see them.

Community bankers: is keeping the savings rate off the website a pricing decision at your bank, or a habit nobody has revisited?
