Addition Financial's loan book is $355 million smaller than the two credit unions that formed it held two years ago.

Envision Credit Union of Tallahassee merged into the Lake Mary credit union on October 1, 2025. On September 30, 2026, the combined credit union held $2.12 billion in loans, down from $2.47 billion in September 2024.

Over the same two years, member shares rose 9% to $3.41 billion, and membership rose 5.2% to 262,040.

The decline is almost all consumer. Vehicle loans fell 26.5% and other unsecured lending fell 26%.

Much of it was deliberate. Addition sold $149.9 million of loan participations in the first nine months of 2025, and its indirect book fell from $505.0 million to $373.0 million.

Lending activity is actually up: $456.9 million of loans granted in the first nine months of 2026, more than the two granted separately a year earlier. Payoffs are outrunning new loans.

The result is a 62.1% loan-to-share ratio. Florida credit unions averaged 85.2% at June 30.

When funding is plentiful and the dealer channel is shrinking on purpose, what actually limits loan growth at your institution?
