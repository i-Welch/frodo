14 of the 27 applications waiting on FDIC deposit insurance come from fintechs and consumer lenders.

PayPal, Klarna, Albert, Flex and OneMain want Utah industrial bank charters. Affirm and Upgrade chose Nevada.

Nubank, Revolut, Upstart, OpenReserve, Mission Lane, Avant and Stark Bank went to the OCC for national charters.

The OCC is moving fast. Five have preliminary conditional approval, and the decision letters set the price of entry: Upstart must hold at least $404.1 million and a 12% leverage ratio. Revolut, $95 million. Mission Lane, $35 million for a subprime card bank.

None can open until the FDIC approves deposit insurance, and OneMain has been waiting more than 19 months.

The business case is funding. Most of these companies rent a bank today through partners like Celtic, Cross River, Lead and WebBank. A charter lets them fund loans with their own insured deposits.

For community bankers: if PayPal offers savings inside an app your customers already open every week, what keeps the deposit with you?
