For 58 days this summer, a Georgia county of 226,568 people had no bank headquartered in it.

On August 1, Peach State Bank & Trust, $786 million in assets, became part of United Community Bank of Greenville, SC. It was the last bank based in Hall County.

On September 28, Private Bank & Trust opened in Gainesville.

What the FDIC data shows:

Hall County's deposits grew from $2.59 billion in 2008 to $5.85 billion in 2026. Bank offices fell from 61 to 38.

Five banks headquartered elsewhere now hold 77% of those deposits. United Community alone, with Peach State folded in, holds 27.4%.

Since 2008, every bank headquartered in the county has been sold: Gainesville Bank & Trust to SunTrust, Chattahoochee Bank of Georgia to Entegra, Peach State to United Community.

The new bank's investors put in more than $33 million, above the offering's original maximum target and well over the FDIC's $19 million floor. Its CEO worked at two of the banks that sold.

The demand for a hometown bank never left. The banks did.

What would it take for a de novo like this one to stay independent?
