In 2008, 21 banks were headquartered in Hillsborough County, Florida. Today there are five, and together they hold 6.4% of the county's deposits.

The money never left. County deposits grew from $20.2 billion to $45.05 billion over the same period. Truist, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Chase hold 53.1% of it.

On September 28, the FDIC approved deposit insurance for New South Bank in Tampa. If it raises $25 million, it will be the first bank chartered in the county since 2007.

What stands out:

The CEO, Kennard Page, spent more than 35 years at the OCC. Another director was an OCC examiner in Tampa until 2025. The people who used to write exam findings will now have to answer them.

The plan targets $250 million in assets by year three. Page once ran Lafayette State Bank, which holds $252.2 million today.

The lending focus is SBA and USDA-guaranteed loans. That guarantee is only as good as the loan file behind it.

Community bankers: if you were opening a bank in Tampa today, what would you build first?
