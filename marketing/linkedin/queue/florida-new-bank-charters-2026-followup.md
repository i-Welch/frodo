From 1995 through 2009, the FDIC insured 228 new institutions headquartered in Florida. Twenty-five in 2007 alone.

From 2012 through 2016, it insured none.

The drought broke in 2017, and the recent class has done well. The nine Florida de novos from 2018 through 2025 that are still operating held $4.2 billion in assets at June 30. Climate First Bank in St. Petersburg opened in 2021 and now holds $1.94 billion.

So demand was never the problem. The problem is the count: two new banks this year will not replace the charters Florida keeps selling.

What would it take to get Florida back to five new banks a year?
