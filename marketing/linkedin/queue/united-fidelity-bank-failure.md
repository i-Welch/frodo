On June 30, United Fidelity Bank reported $16.7 million of noncurrent loans. 0.49% of its book.

On September 30 the number was $2.16 billion. 70.6%.

On November 27 the OCC closed it. First Citizens of Raleigh took the deposits and the branches. The FDIC expects the Deposit Insurance Fund to lose $1.83 billion, about 31% of the bank's $5.9 billion in assets.

That is the largest failure since First Republic in 2023, and more than twice what Republic First cost the fund in 2024.

The warning signs were in the call reports for years:

89% of loans secured by multifamily property.
Multifamily loans alone at 428% of tier 1 capital plus reserves, against the 300% screen regulators use for all CRE.
Negative net interest income every year from 2023. The bank paid more for its money than its loans earned and lived on fee income, which fell every year.
An OCC consent order in 2023 and a Fed written agreement with the holding companies in 2024.

The failure took one quarter. The setup took three years.

If one loan type is most of your book, how often does your board see it repriced, loan by loan?
