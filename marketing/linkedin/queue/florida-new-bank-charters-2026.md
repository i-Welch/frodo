Two new Florida banks opened in 2026 with $97 million in assets between them. Five Florida bank charters retired this year, holding $8.4 billion.

The comeback is real. Florida has six 2026 deposit insurance filings, more than any other state, and three FDIC approvals, all for community banks: Echelon in Clearwater, Portrait in Winter Park, New South in Tampa.

The exits are bigger. FineMark, Winter Park National, Marine Bank & Trust, Prime Meridian and One Florida all left the FDIC's active list in 2026.

Every one of the five was itself a de novo, founded between 1997 and 2017. Florida is not losing old family banks. It is selling the last de novo wave.

Three of the five buyers were credit unions, which took $2.44 billion of Florida bank assets. Four of the five buyers are based outside the state.

Florida had 308 FDIC-insured institutions at the end of 2008. It has 81 today, three fewer than at the start of the year.

Florida bankers: if the most likely end for a successful de novo is a sale, what should a new bank build differently on day one?
