$25 million in capital against a $250 million balance sheet is a 10% leverage ratio, before a single dollar of startup losses.

That is the math behind New South Bank, the Tampa de novo the FDIC approved on September 28. FDIC policy requires a new bank to hold an 8% Tier 1 leverage ratio for its first three years. Every dollar lost before profitability comes out of that two-point cushion.

The business plan aims for profitability in year two, with $225 million in deposits and $180 million in loans the year after. That is an 80% loan-to-deposit ratio.

A thin margin like that rewards a bank that keeps its losses low from day one.
