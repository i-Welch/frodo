Farmers and Merchants Bank of South Carolina lends out only 34.7% of its deposits. It still earned a 1.80% return on assets in the first half of 2026.

The reason is funding. Its interest expense was 0.27% of earning assets, against a 1.59% median for South Carolina banks, and 32.3% of its deposits pay no interest.

First Capital Bank in Charleston makes the state's efficiency top ten the opposite way. It pays 2.97% for funding, nearly twice the state median, and keeps overhead low instead: $13.5 million of assets per employee and four offices for $1.32 billion.

Two models, both among South Carolina's ten most efficient community banks. Neither depends on size.

Which lever is easier for your bank to pull: cheaper deposits or less work per loan?
