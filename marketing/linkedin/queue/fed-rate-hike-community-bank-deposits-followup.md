279 community banks in the Carolinas, Georgia and Florida held $50.9 billion in noninterest-bearing deposits at June 30.

That is 25.4% of their domestic deposits, down from 25.7% a year earlier. At a 4.70% 2-year Treasury yield, that money is worth about $2.4 billion a year to whoever holds it.

Every operating account that leaves gets replaced with a CD or a borrowing at today's prices.

Mortgages show the same pressure from the other side. Freddie Mac's 30-year average rose 64 basis points in the four weeks to October 8. A borrower who didn't lock at a lender that takes 42 days to close carried that whole move.

When rates move this fast, speed shows up in the price.

Where does your bank lose more today: deposits walking out, or loans taking too long to close?
