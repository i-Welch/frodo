53% of the deposits at the Georgia bank Community First Credit Union just bought were in Florida.

First Southern Bank was chartered in 1907 and headquartered in Waycross. But in the FDIC's June 2025 deposit survey, its largest office was in Sebring, Florida, with $82.7 million.

The Jacksonville credit union closed the deal on October 2. FSBH stockholders got $17.14 a share in cash, about $51 million, roughly 1.7 times the bank's equity.

What it bought was funding. $103.2 million of First Southern's $312.9 million in deposits paid no interest at all.

And business lending. 69% of the bank's $226.5 million loan book was business and commercial property credit. At Community First, commercial loans are 10.1% of the book.

Federal rules cap most credit unions' business lending at no more than 12.25% of assets. Community First's low-income designation exempts it.

The gap is the business front door: business checking can't be opened online, and commercial real estate has no online application.

Community bankers: if a credit union will pay 1.7 times equity for your noninterest-bearing deposits and business relationships, how hard would they be to move?
