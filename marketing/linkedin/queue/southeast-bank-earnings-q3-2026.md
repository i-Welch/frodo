Three of the Southeast's largest banks took big losses on purpose last quarter.

Truist took a $1.4 billion pretax loss selling securities. United Community took $366.1 million repositioning its portfolio. First Horizon booked $473 million on a pending sale of about $4.5 billion of lower-yielding mortgages.

Strip those out and all three had ordinary quarters. Truist still earned $684 million for common shareholders.

The community banks did not need a reset. They widened margins instead. Southern First went from 2.62% to 3.02% in a year as wholesale deposits fell 32%. ServisFirst went from 3.09% to 3.63%. First Community, from 3.27% to 3.59%.

The clearest case: customer deposits replaced wholesale deposits.

For community bankers: how much of your funding could you replace with operating accounts if opening one took five minutes?
