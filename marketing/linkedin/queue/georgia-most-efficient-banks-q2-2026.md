The median Georgia bank spent 59.4 cents to earn a dollar of revenue in the first half of 2026. The state's ten most efficient community banks spent 40.6 cents.

We pulled the June 30 FDIC call reports for all 122 banks headquartered in Georgia to see what separates them.

It is not pay. The leaders spend about $111,000 per employee on salaries and benefits, against $103,000 statewide. They pay more.

They need fewer people. The leaders run $9.5 million of assets per employee, against $6.8 million. On a $334 million bank, the state's median size, that is about 35 people instead of 49.

Overhead runs 2.04% of assets at the leaders and 2.78% at the median bank. On that $334 million bank, the gap is worth about $2.5 million a year.

Cheap deposits matter less than in South Carolina. At the median leader, 27.8% of deposits pay no interest, against 25.3% statewide.

And it is not one good quarter. All ten ran an efficiency ratio of 50.3% or better at every mark we checked since 2023.
