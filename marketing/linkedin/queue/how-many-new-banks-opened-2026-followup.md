The eight community banks that opened in 2026 hold four dollars of equity for every dollar of loans.

At September 30 they had $244.8 million of equity, $234.6 million of deposits and $60.6 million of net loans, with 165 employees between them.

All nine of the year's new banks reported a loss for the year to date. That is normal in a first year, when the costs of opening arrive before the loan book does.

The capital is raised. The balance sheets are not built. That work happens during the three-year de novo period, when examiners check whether the programs in the business plan actually exist.

With no branch network to lean on, much of it comes down to the core, the loan system, online account opening and verification chosen before opening day.
