The median South Carolina bank spent 62.6 cents to earn a dollar of revenue in the first half of 2026. The state's ten most efficient community banks spent 50.9 cents.

We pulled the June 30 FDIC call reports for all 43 banks headquartered in the state to see what separates them.

It is mostly cost, not margin. The leaders' median net interest margin is 3.83%, against 3.68%. Their noninterest expense is 2.16% of assets, against 2.66%.

On a $571 million bank, the state's median size, that 50 basis point gap is worth about $2.9 million a year.

They do not pay their people less. Salaries and benefits run about $98,000 per employee at the leaders and $100,000 statewide. The leaders simply run $7.9 million of assets per employee instead of $7.0 million.

They also pay less for funding. At the median leader, 27.1% of deposits pay no interest, against 21.7% statewide.

Size is not the explanation. The leaders range from $318 million to $2.0 billion in assets, and the smallest, Bank of York, runs a 48.8% efficiency ratio with 34 employees.
